# Efficient Inference and Small Language Models: A Survey of Methods, Evidence, and Deployment Realities

## TL;DR

- Efficiency has become a memory-bandwidth problem at least as much as a FLOP problem: for Llama-3-70B in BF16 the 140 GB weight footprint alone exceeds the 80 GB HBM of a single accelerator, and the KV cache of one 128k-token sequence adds 42 GB on top [1]. The same source argues there is a hardware-specific crossover context length below which KV compression produces negligible end-to-end speedup [1].
- Sub-4-bit weight quantization is the most thoroughly quantified lever: FlatQuant reports under 1% accuracy drop for W4A4 on LLaMA-3-70B with up to 2.3x prefill and 1.7x decoding speedups [2], Squeeze10-LLM reaches an average of 1.6 bits per weight [3], PTQ1.61 reaches 1.61 bits [4], and Bit-by-Bit's W2A2 quantization-aware training reports up to 11x speedup over BF16 [5].
- KV-cache compression buys the largest long-context wins but also shows the sharpest quality cliffs: CommVQ cuts FP16 KV cache size by 87.5% at 2-bit quantization, enabling a 128K-context LLaMA-3.1-8B on a single RTX 4090 [6], and RocketKV reports up to 400x compression with 3.7x decode speedup on an A100 [7] - yet naive 1-bit keys and values drop RULER accuracy on Llama-3.1-8B from 84.2% to 47.8% [8].
- Small models are produced by data and distillation budgets rather than raw scale: SmolLM2 (1.7B) is overtrained on roughly 11T tokens with purpose-built datasets [9], while Ministral 3 derives 3B/8B/14B models from a 24B parent using 1-3T tokens instead of the 15-36T tokens cited for Llama3 and Qwen3 [10].
- Deployment evidence is harsher than benchmark evidence: 7B is described as the practical upper limit for a high-end phone and a 4-bit TinyLlama 1B already takes more than 50% of an iPhone 14's 6 GiB RAM [11], sustained iPhone power reaches 13.8 W [12], and SLM-Bench finds no single small model leading accuracy, compute and energy at once [13].

## Background

Efficiency research starts from a diagnosis rather than a single technique. One widely used survey identifies three primary causes of inefficient LLM inference - the large model size, the quadratic-complexity attention operation, and the auto-regressive decoding approach - and organizes the literature into data-level, model-level and system-level optimizations [14]. That three-way split recurs throughout the work reviewed here, and the boundary between them is exactly where the interesting disagreements live.

"Small" is not a stable quantity. One survey defines small language models (SLMs) not by a parameter threshold but by constraints: models that "retain the accuracy and/or adaptability of large language models, while being subject to some constraint(s), such as training or inference hardware, data availability, bandwidth, or generation time" [15]. The same source makes the relativism explicit, noting that GPT-2, a "large language model" in 2019 at 1.5B parameters, is smaller than many "small" language models covered in that survey [15]. A companion survey proposes bounding SLMs from below by the minimal size for emergent abilities and from above by the maximum size sustainable under resource constraints, because studies variously place the boundary below 1B or as high as 10B parameters [16]. Measurement studies settle for operational scopes instead: transformer-based decoder-only models between 100M and 5B parameters, covering devices "from low-end IoT/wearable gadgets like smartwatches to high-end mobile devices" [17][18], or simply "a few hundred million to a few billion parameters" [19].

The substrate is shared with general LLM research. Weight-only quantization is anchored by AWQ, which protects only 1% of salient weight channels selected by observing activations and provides a "turn-key solution to compress LLMs to 3/4 bits" with 1.45x speedup over GPTQ and 1.85x over a cuBLAS FP16 implementation [20]. On the serving side, PagedAttention borrows virtual-memory paging for the KV cache and reports 2-4x throughput at the same latency as prior systems, with the improvement more pronounced for longer sequences and larger models [21]. For decoding, EAGLE drafts at the feature level and claims lossless acceleration - 3x faster than vanilla decoding on MT-bench and an average 160 tokens/s for LLaMA2-Chat 13B on a single RTX 3090 [22]. These three results (a quantization recipe, a memory manager, a drafting scheme) define the axes along which the rest of the field differentiates itself.

## Weight and activation quantization: how low can bit-widths go

The clearest quantitative progress is in weight-only and weight-activation post-training quantization (PTQ). QuIP# targets the extreme regime of at most 4 bits per weight using randomized Hadamard transforms, E8 lattice codebooks and fine-tuning, and reports that its 3-bit models scale better than theoretically lossless 4-bit models - framed by the authors as "a previously unseen result"; its stated motivation is that the largest Llama-2 model (70B) needs 140 GB of GPU memory at native 16-bit precision [23]. The 2-bit frontier is contested by methods with very different mechanisms: PTQ1.61 introduces a one-dimensional structured mask costing a negligible 0.0002 bit per weight to route salient channels to 4-bit precision [4], while Squeeze10-LLM uses staged mixed precision with 80% of weights at 1 bit and 20% at 4 bits for an average of 1.6 bits, lifting average accuracy from 43% to 56% across six zero-shot classification tasks on LLaMA and LLaMA2 relative to existing PTQ methods [3]. TWLA pushes the same direction on the activation side, reporting ternary weights with 4-bit activations (W1.58A4) while noting that prior ternarization work kept activations in high precision, which "fundamentally limit[s] end-to-end inference acceleration" [24]. The harder problem is quantizing activations as aggressively as weights, where naive per-tensor scaling fails; FlatQuant flattens weights and activations by calibrating an optimal affine transformation per linear layer within hours, using Kronecker-product decomposition to keep the overhead low, and reports under 1% accuracy drop for W4A4 on LLaMA-3-70B - surpassing SpinQuant by 7.5% - together with up to 2.3x prefill and 1.7x decoding speedups [2].

A complementary line argues that the quantizer itself is a plug-in component. ADMM-Q casts weight quantization as a combinatorial variant of ADMM with convergence guarantees and is presented as a drop-in replacement for any weight quantizer, composable with range clipping, rotations and activation scaling; on Qwen3-8B WikiText-2 perplexity it improves W3A16 from 12.85 to 10.06 versus GPTQ, and the aggressive W2A4KV4 SpinQuant pipeline from 66.11 to 19.42 [25]. Notably, ADMM-Q's motivating claim is that GPTQ and round-to-nearest "suffer in model utility, especially at aggressive quantization levels (sub-4-bit)" [25] - a direct statement that the 4-bit-optimized tooling of two years earlier does not extrapolate.

Quantization-aware training (QAT) addresses the regime where PTQ degrades. Bit-by-Bit combines block-wise progressive precision reduction, a nested integer quantization grid that allows "train once, deploy any precision", and rounding-aware outlier channel splitting, reporting custom W2A2 operators up to 11x faster than BF16 and a WikiText2 perplexity loss of only 2.25 under W2A2 [5]. The same idea has moved into reinforcement-learning post-training: TRACE uses rollout-guided QAT so that rollout-side quantization outcomes guide training-side FP4 rounding, enabling joint FP4 weight/activation and FP4 KV-cache rollout for Mixture-of-Experts models with up to 5.4x rollout speedup on four large-scale MoE models [26]. Reporting quality varies sharply across this literature: as one note in this survey's evidence base records, most sub-4-bit accuracy numbers are self-reported, benchmark protocols differ (zero-shot classification vs. WikiText-2 perplexity vs. RULER), and no independent head-to-head replication was retrieved.

Structured pruning is the least quantified of the compression families. NIRVANA removes whole hidden units using a first-order saliency criterion derived from the Neural Tangent Kernel under Adam dynamics with adaptive sparsity allocation and KL-based calibration selection, and reports outperforming existing structured pruning methods under equivalent sparsity constraints on Llama3, Qwen and T5 - but without a headline number [27]. A correlation-aware method criticizes pruning research for assessing prunable units in isolation under an implicit additivity assumption and reformulates the problem as a cardinality-constrained binary quadratic program [28]. DarwinLM instead searches for the compressed model with an evolutionary process that accounts for post-compression training, and reports surpassing ShearedLlama with 5x less training data on Llama-2-7B, Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen-2.5-14B-Instruct [29]. The pattern is consistent: pruning papers report accuracy/sparsity trade-offs far more reliably than latency, which is why the compression literature's headline speedups cluster in quantization.

## The KV cache as the real memory wall

At long context, the KV cache stops being an implementation detail and becomes the binding constraint. A systematization-of-knowledge paper states that long-context autoregressive inference "is bounded by memory bandwidth rather than arithmetic throughput" and that the binding resource shifts from model weights to the KV cache as sequence length grows, quantified by the 140 GB BF16 weight footprint of Llama-3-70B against 42 GB of KV for a single 128k-token sequence [1]. Its central structural claim is a three-regime model: below a hardware-specific crossover context length weight traffic dominates and KV compression yields negligible end-to-end speedup; beyond it KV traffic dominates and each technique trades a measurable quality delta for bandwidth savings approaching the roofline bound [1]. The same paper warns that reported gains use "mutually inconsistent workloads, hardware targets, and quality metrics, which prevents rigorous cross-paper comparison" [1], and an independent KV-cache survey corroborates the spread - speedups from roughly 1.25x to 2.87x depending on model, sequence length, batch size and hardware [30].

Within that frame, the compression methods split into eviction, quantization, and hybrids. RocketKV is training-free and two-stage, combining permanent coarse-grain eviction with hybrid sparse attention, and reports up to 400x compression, up to 3.7x end-to-end speedup and up to 32.6% peak memory reduction in the decode phase on an NVIDIA A100, with the scale of the problem illustrated as roughly 320 GB of KV cache for Llama3.1-70B-Instruct at batch 32 and 32K context [7]. RDKV reframes eviction and quantization as endpoints of a single bit-allocation problem solved by reverse water-filling after prefill, reporting 97.81% of full-cache LongBench accuracy at only 2.48% cache retention, plus 4.5x decode speedup and 1.9x peak memory reduction at 128K [31]. Zipage merges token-wise eviction with PagedAttention and adds prefix caching and asynchronous compression for continuous batching, reporting around 95% of full-KV engine performance with over 2.1x speedup on large-scale mathematical reasoning [32]. MAC-Attention attacks the same IO bottleneck from the opposite direction, reusing prior attention computations for semantically similar recent queries so that on a match hit "the compute and bandwidth complexity is constant regardless of context length", reporting up to 99% fewer KV accesses, over 60% lower token generation latency at 128K, and up to 2.6x end-to-end speedup against FlashInfer while maintaining full-attention quality [33].

KV quantization is where the quality cliff is sharpest and best documented. CommVQ designs a RoPE-commutative codebook trained with EM so that decoding reduces to matrix multiplication; it reduces FP16 KV cache size by 87.5% at 2-bit while outperforming state-of-the-art KV quantization methods on long-context benchmarks and GSM8K, and supports 1-bit KV cache with minimal accuracy loss so that a LLaMA-3.1 8B model runs at 128K context on a single RTX 4090 [6]. WUSH-KV derives a data-aware transform from second-order statistics of both factors of a matrix product, folds the value transform into weights and applies the key transform after RoPE, and at 2 bits matches or beats the OSCAR transform across evaluated models and tasks when integrated into SGLang [34]. DGAP quantifies the failure mode that motivates this care: under K1V1 the RULER accuracy of Llama-3.1-8B falls from 84.2% to 47.8%, which the authors diagnose as structured local misranking rather than absolute logit error, and recover to 83.2% by restoring only the selected top-K candidate distribution before token selection [8]. A related shift is architectural: STEPQuant notes that linear attention replaces growing KV caches with fixed-size recurrent states that still bottleneck concurrent serving, and reports matching FP32-state accuracy under a nominal 6-bit budget, over 5x recurrent-state compression and up to 68.7% lower total serving memory in SGLang [35].

## Decoding, speculation, and serving systems

Serving frameworks attack the problem by managing memory rather than shrinking it. PagedAttention's near-zero KV waste and flexible sharing delivers the 2-4x throughput result that made it the reference baseline [21]. LoongServe's elastic sequence parallelism adapts parallelism degree to request and phase variance, reporting up to 3.85x throughput over chunked prefill and 5.81x over prefill-decoding disaggregation [36]. DynaServe splits requests at any token boundary into "micro-requests" with a two-level scheduler and chunked KV transfers, targeting 100 ms P99 time-between-tokens and reporting 1.15x-3.07x serving capacity, up to 1.91x and 1.61x goodput and up to 60% improvement in a hybrid workload - explicitly conditioned on unbalanced and dynamic workloads under SLO constraints [37]. SparseEngine pushes the same idea further by making sparsity the engine's organizing principle rather than an add-on, with a shared lifecycle contract letting 15 methods across four categories control their own KV representation; it reports over 10x higher throughput with KV eviction, over 2.5x faster decoding at matched concurrency than vLLM, and over 2x end-to-end speedup on agent benchmarks [38].

Speculative decoding has become the default way to spend spare compute to save bandwidth. Dustin targets the verification bottleneck where KV cache loading dominates latency, using draft-model lookahead plus historical target-model attention restricted to a minimal subset of attention heads, and reports 27.85x self-attention speedup and 9.17x end-to-end decoding speedup at 32k sequence length with Qwen2.5-72B on PG-19 and LongBench at negligible accuracy cost [39]. BitNest instead makes the draft free by nesting a low-precision draft inside the higher-precision target representation, reaching an average speculative acceptance rate of 95.2% and 1.48-1.61x end-to-end speedup over FP16 on 7B-8B edge-friendly models [40]. SpecStream targets KV offload to CPU memory and starts verification without waiting for the full KV history to be restored, reporting average throughput speedups of 1.41x and 1.32x over the offloading baseline for Qwen3 and InternLM2.5 and 55.4% more output throughput per GPU than parallel speculative decoding on separate target and draft GPUs [41]. The mechanism generalizes beyond autoregressive transformers: SpecFold folds multi-branch computational redundancy in diffusion language models with folded attention and FFN plus a Triton kernel, reporting up to 1.64x throughput over Spiffy and 1.99x over vanilla decoding across two DLLM families and five benchmarks [42]. Notably, one of the most recent, smallest models embeds this capability in the architecture: IronLLM-0.6B, a 654M-parameter on-device model, uses a shared-KV multi-token-prediction design that avoids per-depth KV-cache replay and reports a 1.48x decoding speedup [43].

Mixture-of-Experts introduces a second kind of asymmetry. SlimWise observes that conventional expert pruning also prunes the compute-bound prefill phase "for little throughput benefit" and instead prefill with the full model and decode with a pruned one, reusing the prefill-generated KV cache without conversion and adding a low-cost distillation stage for the decoder; on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B it reports up to 1.81x decode throughput at 50% expert pruning with minimal accuracy loss [44]. Energy measurements confirm the intuition behind such asymmetric designs: sparse routing that activates only two experts per token cuts energy per token by 2-3x versus dense models of similar emergent accuracy, and Mixtral-8x7B consumes roughly the same energy per token as a dense 8B model while delivering quality closer to a dense 56B model [45]. A cross-cutting survey of KV compression adds the useful negative result that these techniques are most effective for input-heavy rather than generation-heavy tasks, and that latency types (prefilling, end-to-end, time-to-first-token, next-token generation) must be compared like with like [30].

## Small language models: data-centric training versus distillation

Two recipes dominate recent SLM releases. The first is aggressive over-training on curated data. SmolLM2 is a 1.7B model overtrained on roughly 11 trillion tokens in a multi-stage process whose mixing rates are updated from the previous stage's performance, with new datasets (FineMath, Stack-Edu, SmolTalk) created where existing data was "problematically small or low-quality", and is claimed to outperform Qwen2.5-1.5B and Llama3.2-1B [9]. TinyLlama established the cheaper version of the recipe: a 1.1B model pretrained on about 1 trillion tokens for approximately 3 epochs on the Llama 2 architecture and tokenizer, using FlashAttention for computational efficiency [46]. Xmodel-2.5 adds hyperparameter transfer and optimizer scheduling to the recipe: 1.3B parameters trained with maximal-update parameterization so that hyperparameters tuned on a 20M-parameter proxy transfer to the full model, on a 1.4T-token Warmup-Stable-Decay curriculum, where switching from AdamW to Muon in the decay phase improved a 13-task reasoning average by 4.58% with all other hyperparameters fixed; the paper page reports the second-best average in the 1-2B range on those 13 benchmarks while using 25.7x less data than leading models [47]. IronLLM-0.6B scales the same approach down further, pretraining a 654M model on approximately 6.2 trillion tokens [43].

The second recipe is distillation from a larger teacher, sometimes with pruning folded in. Ministral 3 uses "Cascade Distillation" - iterative pruning and continued training with distillation - to derive 3B, 8B and 14B dense models from the 24B Mistral Small 3.1 parent, training on 1-3 trillion tokens against 36T for Qwen3 and 15T for Llama3, and reports that the 14B Base closely matches its parent while being more than 40% smaller [10]. DistilQwen2.5 uses proprietary LLMs of varying capacity as multi-agent teachers that select, rewrite and refine instruction-response pairs for the student, followed by model fusion so students integrate fine-grained hidden knowledge [48]; the successor industrial series adds slow-thinking, adaptive-thinking and distilled reward-model variants, the last of which enable further RL of reasoning models, and is deployed on Alibaba Cloud PAI [49]. Domain-specific distillation follows the same two-stage pattern: MedThink has a teacher screen data and inject student-facing knowledge explanations, then evaluate student errors and generate corrective reasoning chains, reporting up to 12.7% improvement over the student baseline on general medical tasks and 56.4% total top accuracy on a 955-question gastroenterology set [50].

Distillation is also where the evidence is weakest. A "Small Model Learnability Gap" result finds that models at or below 3B parameters do not consistently benefit from long chain-of-thought reasoning or from distillation of strong reasoners, performing better on shorter chains aligned with their intrinsic capacity, and proposes Mix Distillation of long and short chains [51]. A separate multi-seed study of the same idea is more alarming: on a 740-instance healthcare API routing task with a 1.5B Qwen student and a 20B teacher, per-seed standard deviation ranged from 2.8 to 48.7 percentage points, "swallowing every claimed KD gain below five points"; three of seven variants showed bimodal collapse with at least one in three-to-five seeds below 55% accuracy, and a naive cross-split +3.78 pp gain reversed to -2.70 pp under controlled within-split re-testing [52]. Method work continues regardless: a cross-tokenizer on-policy distillation study finds that restricting reverse KL to a student-selected top-16 subset of the shared vocabulary at strictly aligned positions matches full shared-vocabulary on-policy distillation and beats the evaluated cross-tokenizer baselines, while adding MSE supervision on span log-probabilities in mismatch groups - despite complete supervision coverage - reduces accuracy [53].

A third, younger direction treats reasoning itself as the efficiency target rather than the model. Selection-Based Structured Reasoning reframes generation as selection from pre-specified reusable natural-language candidates scored by likelihood, which allows parallel scoring over a shared context KV cache; on seven multimodal search benchmarks with 2B and 4B models it reports reducing per-turn reasoning latency by over 90% and total per-question inference latency by 28-54% [54]. Versatility at small scale is also being claimed directly: Nanbeige4.1-3B combines point-wise and pair-wise reward modeling, complexity-aware code rewards and turn-level supervision for long-horizon tool use up to 600 tool-call turns, and is reported to outperform prior models of similar scale including Qwen3-4B and even Qwen3-30B-A3B [55].

## On-device deployment, energy, and economics

The gap between benchmark SLMs and deployable SLMs is the most consistent finding in the deployment literature. MobileAIBench restricts evaluation to at most 7B parameters because "we found 7B to be the upper limit of what a high-end phone's hardware can manage (even after quantization)", with only sub-3B models deployable on an iPhone 14; even a 4-bit TinyLlama 1B takes more than 50% of that device's 6 GiB RAM, and the authors conclude that average time-to-first-token above 60 seconds and below 5 input tokens/s make current multimodal models unsuitable for mobile deployment [11]. MELT, described as the first mobile LLM evaluation suite, measures sustained iPhone power draw up to 13.8 W with instantaneous peaks above 18 W, and 0.16-0.21 mWh per token for a 4-bit Zephyr-3B across six prompts; it finds that fused de-quantize and matrix-multiplication kernels consume 97% and 95.7% of total runtime for prefill and decode respectively on a 3-bit Llama-7B under Android, and concludes that "the continuous execution of LLMs remains elusive" because of energy footprint and thermal behavior [12]. A large-scale edge study of 68 SLMs from 24 organizations reports memory footprints between 275MB and 2456MB measured with llama.cpp on a Jetson, finds that model architecture matters more than parameter count for latency below 1B, and argues that decode is memory-bound while prefill is compute-bound and dominates on-device inference - recommending NPUs for prefill and CPUs for decode [56]. The SLM survey reaches a compatible conclusion from a smaller device set, noting that many SLMs already ship in commercial products and citing third-party results such as PowerInfer-2 serving a 47B model at 11.68 tokens per second on a smartphone with up to a 27.8x speed improvement [18]. Not every workload should move to the edge: a benchmark study of single edge devices and distributed edge clusters finds scenarios where edge inference offers comparable performance at lower cost and others where cloud fallback is essential because of scalability or model-capacity limits [57].

Energy and cost benchmarks make the trade-offs explicit. From Words to Watts reports roughly 3-4 joules per output token at generation length 512 for LLaMA models, with energy per second for LLaMA 65B on the order of 300 W to 1 kW as sharding goes from 8 to 32 GPUs, and notes that more shards always increase both energy per second and energy per output token [58]. TokenPowerBench measures a 7.3x increase in energy per token when moving from LLaMA-3 1B to 70B - far less than the 70x parameter growth - attributes it to cache-bandwidth and memory-traffic penalties beyond FLOPs, reports 25-40% lower energy per token for TensorRT-LLM and vLLM relative to the Transformers engine, about 25% lower per-token energy when raising batch size from 32 to 256, and roughly tripled energy per token when going from 2K to 10K tokens for Llama3 70B [45]. ML.ENERGY Benchmark, covering 40 model architectures across six tasks on A100 and H100 instances, finds that targeting a 100 ms average time per output token lands on the Pareto frontier at 77 ms average TPOT and cuts energy per generation by 44% compared with simply minimizing latency, and that automated optimization recommendations can yield sometimes more than 40% energy savings without changing what is computed [59].

Aggregate comparisons show no free lunch. SLM-Bench evaluates 15 SLMs under 7B parameters on 9 NLP tasks, 23 datasets and 4 hardware configurations across 11 metrics covering correctness, computation and consumption; Llama-3.2-1B is the most accurate and most gold-medalled but falls short on computation and consumption efficiency, GPT-Neo-1.3B leads computational efficiency, Phi-1.5B is the most energy-efficient, and Mistral-7B is the most balanced - i.e., model choice must follow application requirements rather than a single leaderboard [13]. The survey literature converges on the same taxonomy of constraints and open problems: five pillars of on-device SLM work (compact model design, data-centric training, post-training compression, parameter-efficient adaptation, inference-time optimization) with open challenges in evaluation reliability, safety alignment under resource constraints and hardware fragmentation, plus a call for standardized energy-aware benchmarks [19]; three families of optimization (knowledge distillation, quantization and pruning) with complementary mixture-of-experts and early-exit strategies [60]; and formalised metric mappings from deployment setting to constraint to measured quantity, for example efficient inference to latency and throughput or on-device deployment to peak memory and compression ratio [15]. A cost case study of the same tension is unusually concrete: prompt caching at a 99.3% hit rate cut realized API cost by 88.6% to an effective $0.57 per million tokens, below the $2.83 amortized unit cost of a shared on-premise slice, while dedicated on-premise reservation cost 43.8% more than the cached API - but the local configuration also showed a Fix Commit Ratio of 74.9% versus 45.9%, a quality penalty that the savings must be weighed against [61].

## Trends and open problems

Three shifts are visible across this evidence. First, the unit of optimization has moved from the model to the memory system. The most-cited efficiency results of the last cycle are KV-cache results [6][7][31][33], and the SoK framing that the binding resource shifts from weights to the KV cache as sequence length grows [1] is consistent with serving engines restructuring around KV lifecycle management rather than around FLOPs [38][32]. Second, the axes are being combined rather than competed: hybrid compression now mixes quantization with pruning and distillation [60], rate-distortion formulations unify eviction with quantization under one bit budget [31], engines expose KV representation as a pluggable contract [38], and MoE serving prunes asymmetrically between prefill and decode [44]. Third, quantization has moved into the training loop: progressive QAT with a nested grid [5] and rollout-guided FP4 QAT inside RL post-training [26] both treat low precision as a training-time property, not a post-hoc conversion.

The unresolved problems are mostly about evidence rather than methods. Cross-paper comparison is not currently possible: the SoK paper states plainly that reported gains use mutually inconsistent workloads, hardware targets and quality metrics [1], the KV-cache survey calls for a common baseline and notes that the same method's speedup varies by hardware, baseline model, sequence length and batch size [30], and the multi-seed distillation study shows that reported gains below about five points can sit inside seed variance and that single-seed evaluation cannot detect bimodal collapse [52]. Relatedly, self-reported compression results are not independently replicated across the notes underlying this survey, and naive low-bit schemes degrade discontinuously - below 4-bit for naive KV quantization and discontinuously under eviction on position-sensitive tasks [1], with a concrete -36.4 point RULER drop at K1V1 [8].

The small-model half of the field has its own open questions. Definitional instability persists, with "small" bounded by context and time [15] or by emergent-ability and resource-sustainability bounds [16], which makes cross-paper claims hard to aggregate. Distillation from strong reasoners does not reliably transfer to models at or below 3B parameters, and the fix proposed is to adapt the complexity of the supervision rather than to increase it [51], while the reliability of that supervision itself is under scrutiny: complete coverage of mismatch positions can reduce accuracy relative to a narrow but reliable subset [53]. On-device work is constrained less by accuracy than by physics and fragmentation - thermal and energy limits make continuous execution elusive [12], memory budgets rule out models that benchmarks rate highly [11], architecture must be co-designed with hardware such as NPU-prefill/CPU-decode splits [56], and hardware fragmentation plus evaluation reliability are named as the field's leading open challenges [19]. Finally, the economics are unresolved rather than settled: the only dollar-denominated case study in this evidence base finds the cheaper stack also produces a worse defect-repair ratio [61], which suggests that efficiency and quality are trading against each other along a frontier rather than being jointly maximized by any single configuration.
